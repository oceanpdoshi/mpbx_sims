There is likely mode clipping at longer wavelengths (1.5-1.8um). Espsecially higher order modes. 
At shorter wavelengths, you need to solve w/more eigenvectors to guarantee that you actuall get the TE00 and TM00 modes.

A key note on parallelization, is that the geometry step (RibGeometry -> RibDielectric) is slow.
	So very likely, the outer loop on parallelization (different Superlcloud tasks) will be different sets of geometries.
	Then, you parallelize with some multiprocessing (up to memory limit) across different wavelengths.
	The tricky part, is that the grid may change. So perhaps you chunk things intentionally, or eat the extra cost...
	The same argument for adaptiveness is true for the eigenvectors.
		You need nev = 6 for 1000-1200nm
		You need nev = 8 for 650-1000nm
		You need nev = 12 for 400-650nm.
